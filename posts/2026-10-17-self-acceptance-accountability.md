After "I'm trying my best" went up, one comment stuck with me more than the rest.

The pushback: if you go into training telling yourself "I'm not happy until I change this," you're setting yourself up for self-hatred. You have to be happy with who you are first — only then can you actually plan to improve.

I don't disagree with that. I just think it's answering a different question than the one I was asking.

Here's the difference.

"Will changing this make me happy?" is a bad question. Outsourcing your happiness to a future outcome — the number on the scale, the physique, the PR — almost never delivers what people think it will. Happiness can't be outsourced to an outcome.

But that's not the same question as: "If I say this matters to me, are my actions actually reflecting that?"

> Self-acceptance isn't the absence of accountability.

You can love yourself and still demand more from yourself. You can accept where you are without accepting that you have to stay there.

That's the actual point. Not "will this finally make me happy" — that's a trap either way, yes-or-no. It's "am I lying to myself about what I say I want."

Here's what I think gets missed in the "just be happy where you are first" version of this: happiness and accountability aren't sequential steps, one unlocking the other. They run at the same time.

> You don't have to hate where you are to be committed to where you're going. You just have to be honest about the distance between the two.

That's the whole shift. Not self-hatred disguised as motivation. Not self-acceptance used as a reason to stop asking yourself hard questions. Just an honest read on the gap between the life you say you want and the one your actions are actually building.
