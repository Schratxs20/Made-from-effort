The Decision Before the Design

Replacing equipment without fixing the system usually preserves the same problems at a higher cost.

A fitness facility can look dated, feel intimidating, create operational friction, or fail to support the way members actually train. Those problems rarely come from one machine. They come from the relationship between space, equipment, programming, recovery, staffing, storage, and member experience. [See an anonymized example of a room crowded by equipment](https://www.madefromeffort.com/journal/a-gym-isn-t-a-pile-of-good-equipment).

The audit isolates those issues and organizes them into a practical sequence. It can stand alone as a leadership tool or become the foundation for a full Performance Edge design engagement.

What We Examine

## The Facility as *One Operating System.*

01

### Member Experience

Who uses the facility, who avoids it, peak demand, intimidation points, age and ability ranges, and unmet expectations.

02

### Space + Flow

Training zones, circulation, sightlines, clearances, accessibility, congestion, storage, and coaching positions.

03

### Equipment

Condition, relevance, duplication, serviceability, placement, lifecycle, and alignment with actual member demand.

04

### Programming

Personal training, small-group use, mobility, assessment, active-aging needs, and the relationship between space and staff delivery.

05

### Recovery + Wellness

Opportunities for mobility, treatment, quiet recovery, heat, cold, and other services appropriate to the club.

06

### Club Alignment

How fitness compares with the property’s architecture, hospitality, racquet, golf, dining, and broader member standard.

Common Questions

## Before the *Assessment.*

Does the audit require a full renovation?

No. The purpose is to identify the right level of intervention. Recommendations may include operational corrections, equipment changes, phased improvements, or larger capital work.

Is this tied to a specific equipment brand?

No. Recommendations begin with member needs, training function, operations, space, serviceability, and lifecycle—not a predetermined manufacturer list.

Can the audit work with an existing architect or designer?

Yes. It can give the project team a clear fitness and wellness brief before architectural or interior decisions become difficult to change.

What does it cost?

Each audit is proposed as a fixed-fee engagement after an introductory call. Scope depends on the facility, documentation, stakeholders, travel, and required presentation.
