You Don't Need More Hours. You Need an Audit.

October 8, 2026 · Mindset

Almost every client who feels stuck says some version of the same thing: "I just need to find more time" or "I need to want it more." As if the fix is adding something that isn't there yet.

It almost never is. The fix is usually auditing where the time and effort they already have are actually going.

You don't need more capacity. You need to find out what the capacity you already have is being spent on.

Here's the exercise I'd give anyone who feels like they're putting in work and not seeing it pay off: treat every part of your training week like it has a leverage value. Some things move the needle hard. Some barely move it at all. Almost nobody has ever actually sorted their week into those two piles — they just keep doing all of it, at the same intensity, and wonder why the returns feel flat.

01 / Run Every Low-Leverage Habit Through Four Questions, In Order

Kill it. Is this actually earning its spot? The add-on exercise that's been in the program for a year out of habit, the "cardio" that's too easy to build real capacity and too hard to aid recovery — the gray-zone effort that isn't doing much of anything. If stopping it wouldn't cost you anything real, it was never the thing holding you back.

Make it automatic. Every decision you have to re-make costs you willpower you could've spent elsewhere. A standing session time, a packed bag by the door, meals decided in advance — none of that builds fitness directly, but it stops you from burning energy on logistics that should've been settled once.

Tighten it instead of adding to it. Most people respond to a stall by adding more — more sets, more sessions, more supplements. Usually what's missing isn't volume, it's execution: full range of motion, real tempo, actually training close to failure instead of stopping short. Tightening what you're already doing beats piling more on top of it.

Simplify the decision load. Follow the program as written instead of renegotiating it every session based on how you feel. The energy spent debating whether today's the day you skip something is energy not spent doing the work.

02 / Point What You Free Up at the Thing That Actually Matters

Every bit of time and energy you reclaim from the low-leverage stuff goes straight at the highest-leverage work: the main lift that's actually driving the result, the sleep that's actually letting you recover, the consistency that's actually compounding. Most people don't need another hour in the day. They need the hours they already have doing something that counts.

And this isn't a one-time fix. Run the audit again in a month. The junk effort creeps back in — a new add-on exercise, a new inconsistency, a new thing added "just in case." Catching it early is the whole game.

Your time and energy as a client matter. But what matters more is what they're actually being spent on. Audit it before you assume you need more of either.
